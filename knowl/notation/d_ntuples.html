Definition 1.1.3. Real-valued \(n\)-tuples.

Let \(n\) be a positive integer, and let \(I=\{1,2,\dots, n\}\text{.}\) A real-valued tuple indexed by \(I\) is called an \(n\)-tuple and we call \(n\) the length of the tuple. We will favor bold lower case letters when naming \(n\)-tuples (e.g., \(\boldx\text{,}\) \(\boldy\text{,}\) \(\bolda\text{,}\) \(\boldb\)) and use any of the following notations for tuples indexed by \(I=\{1,2,\dots, n\}\text{:}\)
\begin{align*} (a_i)_{i\in I}\amp\amp (a_i)_{i=1}^n \amp\amp (a_1,a_2,\dots,a_n) \text{.} \end{align*}
Furthermore, the set \(\R^I\) of all \(n\)-tuples is denoted \(\R^n\text{:}\) i.e.,
\begin{equation} \R^n=\{(a_i)_{i=1}^n\mid a_i\in \R \text{ for all } i\in \{1,2,\dots,n\}\}\text{.}\tag{1.1.1} \end{equation}
