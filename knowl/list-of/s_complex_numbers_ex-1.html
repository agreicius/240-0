Exercise 1.

Rewrite the following expression into the form of a+b\(i\text{:}\)
🔗
\(\displaystyle{ \frac{{5-6i}}{{-6-5i}} = }\)
🔗
Answer.
\(i\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
By definition, \(i=\sqrt{-1}\text{,}\) so we have \(i^{2}=-1\text{.}\)
🔗
To get rid of \(i\) in the denominator, we use the difference of squares formula:
🔗
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned} \amp \phantom{{}=}(-6+{-5i})(-6-{-5i}) \\ \amp = (-6)^{2}-({-5i})^{2} \\ \amp = (36)-(-25) \\ \amp = 61 \end{aligned} }\)
🔗
The full solution is:
🔗
\(\displaystyle{ \begin{aligned} \amp \phantom{{}=} \frac{{5-6i}}{{-6-5i}} \\ \amp = \frac{({5-6i})(-6-{-5i})}{({-6-5i})(-6-{-5i})} \\ \amp = \frac{(5)(-6)+(5)(-{-5i})+({-6i})(-6)+({-6i})(-{-5i})}{61} \\ \amp = \frac{(-30)+(25i)+(36i)+(-30)(i^{2})}{61} \\ \amp = \frac{(-30)+(61i)+(-30)(-1)}{61} \\ \amp = \frac{(-30)+(61i)+(30)}{61} \\ \amp = \frac{(0)+(61i)}{61} \\ \amp = \frac{0}{61}+\frac{61i}{61} \\ \amp = {i} \end{aligned} }\)
