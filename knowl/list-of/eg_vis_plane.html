Example 2.1.7. Visualizing planes in \(\R^3\).

Let \(\mathcal{P}\subseteq \R^3\) be a plane defined by the nontrivial equation
\begin{equation} ax+by+cz=d\text{.}\tag{2.1.3} \end{equation}
We will describe two methods of visualizing \(\mathcal{P}\text{.}\) The first, more elementary approach might be described as the triangle method. This entails first finding by inspection three non-colinear points \(P,Q,R\) satisfying equation (2.1.3). The plane \(\mathcal{P}\) is then visualized by sketching the triangle \(\triangle PQR\) in \(\R^3\) they define, and envisioning \(\mathcal{P}\) as the plane in \(\R^3\) containing this triangle. In practice we can find three such points by picking arbitrary values for two of the three variables, and solving for the third variable using (2.1.3).
🔗
For example, if \(\mathcal{P}\) is the plane with equation \(x+y+z=2\text{,}\) we see easily that \(P=(2,0,0), Q=(0,2,0), R=(0,0,2)\) lie on \(\mathcal{P}\text{,}\) allowing us to produce the graph in Figure 2.1.8.
🔗
Leaning on your multivariable calculus background, we also recall a method of visualization using normal vectors. Since the equation (2.1.3) is nontrivial, the vector \(\boldn=(a,b,c)\) is nonzero and is called a normal vector to the plane \(\mathcal{P}\text{.}\) The normal vector method for visualizing \(\mathcal{P}\) proceeds as follows: pick any point \(P=(x_0,y_0,z_0)\in \mathcal{P}\text{,}\) and visualize \(\mathcal{P}\) as the set of all points \(Q=(x,y,z)\) such that the vector \(\overrightarrow{PQ}=(x-x_0, y-y_0, z-z_0)\) is orthogonal to \(\mathcal{n}\text{.}\) This allows us to identify \(\mathcal{P}\) as the plane passing through \(P\) and orthogonal to \(\boldn\text{.}\)
🔗
Note: by invoking orthogonality, we might be accused of having gotten ahead of ourselves somewhat. Although we will take up the notion of orthogonality in full generality in Chapter 7, we are justified in using it in the context of \(\R^3\text{,}\) since this is treated in standard multivariable calculus courses.
