Definition 1.1.17. Special elements of \(\R^I\).

Let \(I\) be a nonempty set.
  • Zero vector.
    The zero vector of \(\R^I\text{,}\) denoted \(\boldzero_I\) (or \(\boldzero\) for short), is the \(I\)-tuple, all of whose entries are equal to \(0\text{:}\) i.e., \(\boldzero_I=(0)_{i\in I}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    In the special cases \(\R^n\) and \(\ML_{m,n}(\R)\text{,}\) we denote the corresponding zero vectors as \(\boldzero_n\) and \(\boldzero_{m\times n}\text{,}\) respectively.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Vector inverse.
    Given an \(I\)-tuple \(\boldx=(x_i)_{i\in I}\in \R^I\text{,}\) the vector inverse of \(\boldx\text{,}\) denoted \(-\boldx\text{,}\) is the element of \(\R^I\) defined as
    \begin{equation*} -\boldx=(-x_i)_{i\in I}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  • Indicator vectors.
    Given \(i\in I\text{,}\) the \(i\)-th indicator element of \(\R^I\text{,}\) denoted \(\bolde_i\text{,}\) is the \(I\)-tuple whose \(i\)-th entry is equal to \(1\) and whose \(j\)-th entry is equal to \(0\) for all \(j\ne i\text{.}\)
    🔗
    In the case \(\R^n\text{,}\) we call \(\bolde_i\) the \(i\)-th standard basis vector of \(\R^n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    In the case \(\ML_{m,n}(\R)\text{,}\) we write \(E_{i,j}\) for \(\bolde_{(i,j)}\text{,}\) and call this the \(i,j\)-th standard basis matrix of \(\ML_{m,n}(\R)\text{.}\)
    🔗
