Definition 1.1.10. \(n\)-tuples and infinite sequences.

  • \(n\)-tuple.
    When \(I=\{1,2,\dots, n\}\) for some positive integer \(n\text{,}\) we will write \(\R^n\) for \(\R^I\text{,}\) and will use any of the following notations for a tuple of \(\R^n\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} (a_i)_{i\in I}\amp\amp (a_i)_{i=1}^n \amp\amp (a_1,a_2,\dots,a_n) \text{.} \end{align*}
    In this case we call \((a_i)_{i=1}^n\) an \(n\)-tuple, and we call \(n\) the length of the \(n\)-tuple \((a_i)_{i\in I}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • Infinite sequences.
    When \(I=\Z_{\geq 1}=\{1,2,\dots\}\text{,}\) we will write \(\R^\infty\) for \(\R^I\text{,}\) and will use any of the following notations for a tuple of \(\R^\infty\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} (a_i)_{i\in I}\amp\amp (a_i)_{i=1}^\infty \amp\amp (a_1,a_2,\dots) \text{.} \end{align*}
    An element \((a_1,a_2,\dots,)\) of \(\R^\infty\) is called an infinite sequence of real numbers.
