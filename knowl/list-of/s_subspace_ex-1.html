Let \(V\) be {\em dyad}: i.e., \(V\) is a set containing exactly two elements. Show that there is no choice of operations
\begin{align*} V\times V \amp \rightarrow V \amp \R\times V\amp \rightarrow V\\ (\boldv,\boldw) \amp \mapsto \boldv+\boldw \amp (c,\boldv)\amp \mapsto c\boldv \end{align*}
turning \(V\) into a vector space. In other words, show that for any choice of such operations, at least one of the axioms of Definition 1.1.16 will fail.
