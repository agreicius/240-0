Example 1.1.18. Zero, inverse, and indicator vectors.

For the given index set \(I\text{,}\) provide (a) the zero vector of \(\R^I\text{,}\) (b) a general element of \(\R^I\) and its vector inverse, and (c) the indicator vectors \(\bolde_i\) for all \(i\in I\text{.}\)
🔗
Make use of the notational conventions that apply to each particular type of index set.
  1. \(I=\{1,2,3,4\}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\displaystyle I=\{(i,j)\mid 1\leq i\leq 2, 1\leq j\leq 3\}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
  1. In this case we have \(\R^I=\R^4\text{.}\) The zero vector of \(\R^4\) is \(\boldzero=(0,0,0,0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    A general element of \(\R^4\) can be described as \(\boldx=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\text{,}\) \(x_i\in \R\text{,}\) and its vector inverse is \(-\boldx=(-x_1,-x_2,-x_3,-x_4)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The four indicator vectors of \(\R^4\) are called standard basis vectors, and are given as
    \begin{align*} \bolde_1 \amp =(1,0,0,0) \amp \bolde_2=(0,1,0,0)\\ \bolde_3 \amp =(0,0,1,0) \amp \bolde_4=(0,0,0,1)\text{.} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. In this case we have \(\R^I=\ML_{2,3}(\R)\text{.}\) The zero vector of \(\ML_{2,3}(\R)\) is \(\boldzero=(0)_{(i,j)\in I}=\begin{amatrix} 0\amp 0\amp 0\\ 0\amp 0\amp 0 \end{amatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    A general element of \(\ML_{2,3}(\R)\) can be described as
    \begin{equation*} A=\begin{amatrix} a_{11}\amp a_{12}\amp a_{13}\\ a_{21}\amp a_{22}\amp a_{23} \end{amatrix}\text{,} \end{equation*}
    and its vector inverse is
    \begin{equation*} -A=\begin{amatrix} -a_{11}\amp -a_{12}\amp -a_{13}\\ -a_{21}\amp -a_{22}\amp -a_{23} \end{amatrix}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    The six indicator vectors of \(\ML_{2,3}(\R)\) are called standard basis vectors, and are given as
    \begin{align*} E_{1,1}\amp= \begin{amatrix} 1\amp 0\amp 0\\ 0\amp 0\amp 0 \end{amatrix} \amp E_{1,2}\amp= \begin{amatrix} 0\amp 1\amp 0\\ 0\amp 0\amp 0 \end{amatrix} \\ E_{1,3}\amp= \begin{amatrix} 0\amp 0\amp 1\\ 0\amp 0\amp 0 \end{amatrix} \amp E_{2,1}\amp= \begin{amatrix} 0\amp 0\amp 0\\ 1\amp 0\amp 0 \end{amatrix} \\ E_{2,2}\amp= \begin{amatrix} 0\amp 0\amp 0\\ 0\amp 1\amp 0 \end{amatrix} \amp E_{2,3}\amp= \begin{amatrix} 0\amp 0\amp 0\\ 0\amp 0\amp 1 \end{amatrix} \text{.} \end{align*}
