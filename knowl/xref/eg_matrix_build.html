Example 1.1.8. Matrices.

Display the given matrix \(A\) as a rectangular array.
  1. \(m=n=2\text{,}\) \(A=[a_{i,j}]_{m\times n}\text{,}\) where \(a_{i,j}=2(i-1)+j\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\displaystyle A=[2(i-1)+j]_{(i,j)\in \{1,2\}\times \{1,2,3\}}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(m=1\text{,}\) \(n=3\text{,}\) \(A=[1]_{m\times n}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. \(m=3\text{,}\) \(n=1\text{,}\) \(A=[1]_{m\times n}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
  1. \(A\) is \(2\times 2\) in this case (two rows, two columns), and we have
    \begin{align*} A \amp = \begin{bmatrix} 2\cdot(1-1)+1 \amp 2(1-1)+2 \\ 2\cdot (2-1)+1 \amp 2(2-1)+2 \end{bmatrix} \\ \amp = \begin{amatrix}[rr] 1\amp 2 \\ 3\amp 4 \end{amatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(A\) is \(2\times 3\) in this case (two rows, three columns), and we have
    \begin{align*} A \amp = \begin{bmatrix} 2\cdot(1-1)+1 \amp 2(1-1)+2 \amp 2(1-1)+3 \\ 2\cdot (2-1)+1 \amp 2(2-1)+2 \amp 2(2-1)+3 \end{bmatrix} \\ \amp = \begin{amatrix}[rrr] 1\amp 2 \amp 3\\ 3\amp 4 \amp 5 \end{amatrix}\text{.} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(A\) is \(1\times 3\) in this case (one row, three columns), and we have
    \begin{align*} A \amp = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} \text{.} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
  4. \(A\) is \(3\times 1\) in this case (three rows, one column), and we have
    \begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
