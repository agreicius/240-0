Definition 1.1.13. Vector operations of \(\R^I\).

Let \(I\) be a nonempty set. We will call the operations below the standard vector operations on \(\R^I\text{.}\)
  • Vector addition on \(\R^I\).
    Given elements \(I\)-tuples \(\boldx=(x_i)_{i\in I}\) and \(\boldy=(y_i)_{i\in I}\text{,}\) we define their vector sum \(\boldx+\boldy\) as
    \begin{equation*} \boldx+\boldy=(x_i)_{i\in I}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    The operation
    \begin{align} \R^I\times \R^I \amp \rightarrow \R^I\tag{1.1.3}\\ (\boldx, \boldy) \amp \mapsto \boldx+\boldy\tag{1.1.4} \end{align}
    is called vector addition.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Scalar multiplication on \(\R^I\).
    Given an \(I\)-tuple \(\boldx=(x_i)_{i\in I}\in\R^I\) and a real number \(c\in \R\text{,}\) the \(I\)-tuple \(c\boldx\) defined as
    \begin{equation*} c\boldx=(cx_i)_{i\in I} \end{equation*}
    is called the scalar multiple of \(\boldx\) by \(c\text{.}\) The operation
    \begin{align} \R\times \R^I \amp \rightarrow \R^I\tag{1.1.5}\\ (c,\boldx) \amp \mapsto c\boldx\tag{1.1.6} \end{align}
    is called scalar multiplication.
